# Chassis cornering dynamics

Cornering is CG location, weight transfer, caster, and chassis stiffness (plus more).

## CG

Treat mass as acting at the CG (X fore-aft, Y vertical, Z lateral).

- **X:** usually just ahead of the seat. Hard to move without redrilling the seat or moving ballast.
- **Y:** taller drivers sit higher; seat height and rear ride-height holes move it a little.
- **Z:** driver lean plus speed/radius set how much load goes to the outside.

## Weight transfer

The kart needs the **inside rear jacked** off the track. Transfer is about the **angle from CG to the contact patch**, not only moving the CG.

- Rear hubs **inboard** → steeper angle → **more jacking**
- Rear hubs **outboard** → less jacking
- Tune until it jacks without **hop**
- Recheck as the track rubbers in or weather changes

**Front is the opposite:** you want **even** front load (a lightly loaded tyre has less grip). Lower CG + wider front track = less front transfer = more front grip. That is one reason for a **wedge**: lower in front than rear.

## Caster jacking

Caster tilts the kingpin rearward. Steer right: left stub wants to lift, right stub wants to press down. Because the frame is stiff, the kart becomes a **three-legged stool** and pivots on **inside-front to outside-rear**, lifting the inside rear. Both fronts should stay planted.

More caster = more jacking as you add lock. Especially useful when **slow** (wet, green) because it is harder to get the inside rear up. Also eases turn-in and adds front grip.

## Stiffness / roll resistance

A kart is a ladder frame with a **narrow waist**, so the front is torsionally softer than the rear. You cannot change tube metal easily, but you can change each **end**.

More roll resistance at the rear (seat stays, tight centre bearing, torsion bar, tight rear bumper, stiffer axle) helps unload the inside rear.

Front: torsion bar raises roll resistance. Wider front track increases steer-jack and front grip / turn-in.

## Balance

Best case on the "curve": inside rear off, all drive through the outside rear. Too stiff hits diminishing returns. Photos show full lift less often than people think. It is a balance.
