## Improves: corner speed

- **Unload the inside rear.** The kart only corners well when the inside rear
  lifts. Technique side: one committed steering input at turn-in (jacking needs
  steer angle), not a series of saw cuts. Excess mid-corner steer scrubs speed.
- **Drive yaw rotates, brake yaw aligns** (validated lap model): the kart
  understeers while braking and rotates under power. Get the slow work done
  early, then use the throttle to finish the rotation.
- **Momentum over V-line in low-power classes** (Cadet, KA, 4SS): rolling speed
  beats point-and-shoot because there's no torque to recover it.
- Look through the corner to the exit — the kart follows the eyes.

## Improves: consistency

- One reference point per phase: brake marker, turn-in, apex, exit kerb.
- Session loop from the KB: run → pyrometer/pressures → ONE change → re-run.
  Driver changes count as the one change too.
- Log every session (chassis, pressures, temps, air/track, gearing) — the
  session-tracking-guide.md format.
- Year one is a grounding year (rookie guidance): master flags, starts, and
  clean laps before chasing tenths.

## Improves: race craft

- Starts: in position by the red line, constant speed, stay in the tramlines —
  jumped starts and filled grid gaps are penalties (see rules KB).
- The corner belongs to the inside kart when its front wheels are at least
  level with the outside kart's sidepod — plan passes to be clearly alongside,
  never a late dive ("cut-in" / "edge-into" offences).
- One defensive move plus one move for the corner is the limit; weaving is
  penalised.
- No payback, no bumping: rear contact that costs the kart ahead a place is
  typically the bumper's fault.

## Improves: wet pace

- Drive OFF the rubbered dry line: square the corners, cross the dry line at
  right angles where possible — the rubber is ice when wet.
- Lean the upper body OUT of the corner to load the outside front (opposite of
  dry instinct).
- Brake earlier and softer — rear-only brakes lock quickly on a wet surface.
- Setup and gearing changes: see wet section of chassis-setup-and-tyre-kb.md
  (+3 rear teeth baseline, max caster, max front width).

## Braking technique (rear-only vs KZ2 front)

- Sprint karts brake with the REAR AXLE ONLY. Peak deceleration is capped
  (~−5.5 m/s² in the validated model) — far below what the tyres could give
  with front brakes. Consequences: brake earlier than instinct says, release
  progressively into turn-in, and never expect car/bike trail-braking depth.
- A rear lockup flat-spots both rears (live axle) — brake in a straight line,
  modulate at the point of squeal, not past it.
- KZ2 has front brakes: braking zones shorten dramatically and light trail
  braking becomes a real tool — but only in KZ2. Never carry KZ2 advice to
  sprint classes.

## Vision + reference points

- Eyes up at the exit before the apex; the hands follow the eyes.
- Pick fixed references (kerb seams, cracks, marshal posts), not other karts.
- In traffic, look past the kart ahead to keep your own line and brake points.

## Kerb use

- Green/cold tyres: stay off kerbs until the tyres are in (cold-tear risk).
- Inside kerbs: flat entry kerbs can be used once the kart is rotated; a kerb
  strike mid-jacking unsettles the lifted inside rear.
- Exit kerbs: use them to open the corner IF flat — dropped wheels and hooked
  edges bend axles and ruin alignment (recheck toe after any big strike).
- Wet: kerbs are paint-slick; treat as off-track.

## Drafting

- A kart tow is worth the most at the end of long straights; break the tow
  before your brake marker so you're not arriving faster than your reference.
- Give a tow to a class rival only when it also helps your lap/heat position;
  in quali, track position and clear air usually beat a draft.
- In heats, work with the kart ahead to break from the pack — swap the lead on
  alternate straights only if both drivers are clean and predictable.
