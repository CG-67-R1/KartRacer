# Tyre setup

Four contact patches about the size of a palm keep the kart on the road. Treat them as the primary sensor.

## Storage and control

Store dark, ~20 °C, low humidity, away from sun, fluorescent light, heat, electric motors (ozone), fuel. Not below 0 °C. Peel the film just before fitment.

CIK barcodes usually start with **8**. Event control: only **your** marked tyres; no swapping between karts.

**Scrub-in (debated):** a few laps to temp, a couple more, pit, cool fully (some use wet rags). New tyres grip more; each heat cycle loses grip. Recheck alignment after kerbs/incidents. Check pressures before and after. Store with excess air removed. **Do not deflate in parc fermé.**

Tyre softeners: safety risk, often toxic, usually only "help" in the wet — and you often have too much dry grip anyway.

## Temperatures

Needle pyrometer under the tread is best; IR is cheaper. Read **outside / middle / inside**. Makers' optimum **75–85 °C**. Near **95 °C** they self-destruct (shiny wet look on track). Ignore absolute numbers first — chase **variance**. All tracks work one side harder; match **front vs rear**, not left vs right. Big L/R split can mean a **bent chassis**.

## Rear (pressure / transfer)

| O / M / I | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 80 / 70 / 80 | Under-inflated |
| 70 / 80 / 70 | Over-inflated |
| 80 / 80 / 80 | Even |
| 70 / 80 / 90 | Not enough weight transfer (may not show as cone yet) |
| 90 / 80 / 70 | Too much transfer — may hop |

## Front (camber then pressure)

Pressure and camber interact: softer pressure → more sidewall roll → need more negative camber.

| O / M / I | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 70 / 80 / 90 | Too much negative camber |
| 90 / 80 / 70 | Too much positive camber |
| 80 / 90 / 80 | Camber OK, over-inflated |
| 90 / 80 / 90 | Camber OK, under-inflated |

## Cone wear

Inner edge eaten, outer unused.

- **Rear:** not enough transfer. Stiffen rear (stays) or **narrow** rear track.
- **Front:** too much camber or too little caster; often with understeer. Widen front **or** more positive camber (steer heavier, release quicker). One change at a time.

Fix early — a cone-shaped profile is permanent.

## Blistering

Overworked rubber melts in bubbles, usually one edge, rarely across the whole face.

## Session loop

1. Run, pyrometer immediately, record.
2. Front: more negative camber if outer hot; less if inner hot. Or more positive caster if understeering.
3. Rear: drop pressure if middle hot; raise if middle cold. Hot inner = more transfer; hot outer = less.
4. Re-run. Then trim front pressures so the middle matches.
5. Use chassis (ride height etc.) to move overall front/rear into the 75–85 °C band.
6. Log chassis, tyre temps, air temp, track temp (pyrometer on asphalt).
