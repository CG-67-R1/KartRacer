# Chassis straightening

Rubbing is racing until the tubes take a **permanent set**. Many bends are recoverable; a **kinked / creased** tube has lost strength — usually buy a new chassis. Do not cut-and-weld mystery tube (unknown alloy, heat-treat, HAZ).

## Material

Steel is elastic up to yield, then plastic (permanent set), then fracture. Fatigue from many small loads also kills elasticity (paper-clip analogy). Makers rarely publish grade/wall/heat-treat. Some (Haase, Tony Kart) say chrome-moly without the grade. Modern frames trend **softer** (more flex, shorter peak-performance life, easier to bend).

Trackside "parallel to the axle" is a **temporary** get-you-out fix.

## Proper process (outline)

Need a rigid table: axle in rear hoops, nose on a centre pivot. Strip bumpers, tank, bib, pods, wheels, engine, seat.

1. Visual + straight-edge in **both** planes — fix big bends first.
2. Square/flat the **bearing carriers** (often bent up).
3. Compare kingpin **heights**; then side lengths axle→kingpin and diagonals carrier→kingpin (crab / short side).
4. Straighten **rear** (tubes, carriers, bumper supports) **before** the front.
5. Front main tube must sit flat on the dome support.
6. Level yoke tops; reset caster on centreline (pin gauges + digital protractor — off-axis = junk numbers).
7. Refit hubs; laser camber and toe (Sniper).

Over-bend into the plastic range with a press and shaped blocks. Amount and location are craft.

## Scales / corner weights

Work to tenths of a millimetre. Verify on four scales on a **levelled** surface (garage floors lie). Driver in full kit, race fuel, wheels centred on pads, steering straight.

Target: **50/50 left-right**, about **43% front / 57% rear**. Move ballast and seat (keep the driver comfortable). Cross-weight: raise a corner (front yoke shims or rear carrier hole) to **take weight off that wheel** and send it diagonally. Small steps.

Some people re-bend a "fixed" chassis to shift cross — crude, and it undoes the straighten.

## How often

Top teams: every 2–3 meetings. Most: once a season. Kerb-hoppers: as often as budget allows. Even a new chassis can gain from an expert check. Soft pads/rags keep scuffs minor — a scuffed straight kart beats a pretty bent one.
