## Reading a kart slick (zones/bands)

Divide the tread face into three bands: **outer shoulder / centre / inner
shoulder** (relative to the kart, not the corner). Judge two things per band:
texture (grain quality) and relative wear/heat. Healthy reference: even fine
sandpaper grain across all three bands, matching front-vs-rear averages.
Smooth glassy face = tyre never reached temperature. Always pair a visual read
with a pyrometer read taken immediately on stopping (75–85 °C band).

## Graining (cold/dirty track)

Torn, dusty, feathered rubber pellets on the surface — the tyre slid before it
came up to temp (green track, cold morning, over-driving out-laps).
Fix order: raise pressure a step to bring the tyre in faster → smoother inputs
on the first laps → add mechanical grip for the green surface (stiffer rear,
struts in, torsion bar in, more caster) → graining usually clears once the
track rubbers in.

## Blistering (overheat)

Melted bubbles or pockmarks, usually on one edge, rarely across the whole face —
overworked rubber, carcass past ~95 °C (shiny wet look on track is the live
warning). Fix order: drop pressure → reduce the load on that end (rear: widen
track / soften axle; front: less caster) → check the driver is not sliding the
kart; a blistered edge does not recover.

## Hot tear vs cold tear

- **Hot tear:** rubber smeared/rolled in soft strips, greasy look — tyre over
  temp or pressure too high for conditions. Lower pressure, remove grip, cool
  the tyre on in-laps.
- **Cold tear (chunking/rip):** sharp-edged torn flakes — tyre worked hard while
  still below temp (cold track + aggressive first laps). Raise pressure, warm
  the tyre progressively, don't attack kerbs until it's in.
Distinguish by touch and timing: hot tear feels greasy after a long run; cold
tear appears on the first laps of a session.

## Flat spots (brake lockup)

Single flat patch, often with a vibration report from the driver. Kart-specific
cause: rear-only brakes in sprint classes mean a lockup flat-spots BOTH rears.
Fix: none for the tyre (rotate to practice sets if allowed); coach the braking
release and check brake-pad drag and pedal free play so it doesn't recur.

## Inner/outer shoulder wear (camber/width)

- **Cone wear** — inner edge eaten, outer unused (the classic kart pattern):
  - Rear: not enough weight transfer → stiffen rear or **narrow** rear track.
  - Front: too much negative camber or too little caster, often with understeer
    → widen front or add positive camber. Fix early; a coned profile is permanent.
- Outer shoulder hot/worn (front): not enough negative camber.
- Aggressive strips on one inner edge: too much caster/camber on that corner or
  that edge overheating.

## One-side wear (toe/alignment)

Both fronts scrubbed evenly across the face with a dragged look = too much
toe-out (also shows as darty on straights and heavy tyre-temp spread). One
whole side of the kart working harder than the other beyond the normal track
bias = corner weights, asymmetric settings, or a **bent/twisted chassis** —
check before touching setup. Kerb strikes and incidents: recheck alignment
before blaming wear on setup.

## Fix order per pattern

Cheap and reversible first:

| Pattern | Fix order |
|---|---|
| Graining | Pressure up → smoother inputs → green-track grip changes |
| Blistering | Pressure down → unload that end → driver check |
| Hot tear | Pressure down → remove grip → cooling laps |
| Cold tear | Pressure up → progressive warm-up |
| Flat spot | Braking technique → pad drag / free play check |
| Cone (rear) | Stiffen rear → narrow rear track |
| Cone (front) | Widen front → positive camber → caster check |
| One-side / both-front scrub | Toe check → corner weights → chassis straightness |
