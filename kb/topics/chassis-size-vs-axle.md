# Chassis size vs axle choice

Standard paddock rule (true for most **1050 mm** seniors): mid-range axle; **stiff** for cold/slippery; **soft** for high grip / rubbered; high-power likes stiff; low-power and tall drivers like softer.

That rule **often fails on 950 mm**.

## Why 950 is different

Torsional stiffness comes from waist **width** and tube **OD**.

| Chassis | Typical main tubes |
|---|---|
| 950 mm (Kid/Mini etc.) | 28 mm, narrower waist → **much softer** torsionally |
| 1050 mm | 30 or 32 mm (Birel exception: RY29 = 28, RY30 = 30, RY32 = 32) |
| Bambino | 770 mm wheelbase — treat separately |

**CRG shape:** short, wide waist → torsionally stiffer.  
**OTK shape:** longer, narrower waist — used by Birel, Energy, Intrepid, Parolin, Ricciardo, Tony, Zanardi (and some Zanardi-built-by-CRG frames). Those should react similarly.

Stiff chassis: twist the front, the rear follows → inside rear lifts easily. Soft chassis: like twisting rubber — inside rear wants to stay down.

On a **soft 950**, a **soft axle** can force the **outside** tyre to work harder and **add side bite**. That is the opposite of the 1050 textbook.

## Slide vs hop

Regardless of size: **sliding rear = add grip**; **hopping rear = reduce grip**.

Green / cold / rain: more grip is useful. Rubbered / hot: less.

## Axle legality

Any maker's axle is usually legal if OD, wall, and class length match. **30 mm class length: 960 ± 10 mm** — trim if needed, never go short.

Axle charts (CRG Brinell HB; OTK 30/40/50) are in the source page.

Other grip levers: driver height, seat, stays, rear track, pressures, hub width, Ackermann. One change at a time. If the book does not work on **your** 950, try the opposite.
