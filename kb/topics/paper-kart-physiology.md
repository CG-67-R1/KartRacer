# Driver physiology

Amateur kart drivers, on-track sensors. Small sample — **preliminary**, not a training prescription.

## What they could measure in a kart

Space, vibration, and arm movement make motorsport physiology hard. They logged:

- Vector acceleration (**G**)
- ECG heart rate
- Wrist blood pressure (before/after)
- Eardrum temperature as core-temp proxy
- Lap time

The kit survived a circuit session.

## Findings

- HR sat near **150 bpm** for all participants while driving.
- Time–frequency analysis of HR pointed to **sympathetic** drive, not just “exercise.”
- In one driver, G and HR were **significantly cross-correlated** — they suggest the HR rise is partly a response to **G load**, not only muscle work. Not enough data to treat that as proven.
- Immediately after driving: **blood pressure down** and **eardrum temperature up** (both p < 0.01 in their tests).

## Pit takeaway

A kart session is a cardiovascular event. Heat soaks the driver; neck/rib protection and hydration are not optional. If someone “feels fine” but fades late, look at fitness and cooling, not only jetting. Do not use 150 bpm as a target — it is what *their* amateurs hit, not a coaching number.
