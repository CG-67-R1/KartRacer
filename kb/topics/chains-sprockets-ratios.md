# Chains, sprockets and ratios

Power sets top speed; **torque at the rear tyres** sets acceleration. Class engines are largely sealed; gearing is the legal torque lever.

## Ratio rules of thumb

- More **front** teeth → less acceleration, more speed range
- More **rear** teeth → more acceleration, less top speed
- Same ratio, smaller pair is better for kerb clearance (example: 90/10 = 81/9 = 9:1)
- **Higher ratio number** = more acceleration, less top speed (like 1st vs 5th)

Do not use extra rear teeth to hide a handling problem. Wet + rain tyres: **+3 or more** rear teeth; drop teeth as it dries.

## Chain

- 4-stroke karts often **#35** (~9.5 mm pitch)
- 2-stroke karts **#219** (7.774 mm): more teeth in a given diameter, ~13% lighter, less vibration, longer life
- Wear is pin/roller elongation. Kart replacement guide ~**3%** elongation (fatigue theory likes ≤1.5%)
- Field check: chain flat, rollers up, Vernier internals across **14 pitches** (~104 mm new). Replace between 75–100% of the wear table in the source article
- Slack **A ≈ 4%** of centre distance → typically ~**10 mm** total up/down on ~250 mm centres
- #219 has **no master link**; sold 94–116 links in steps of 2. One chain-length step ≈ **7.5 mm** engine move; one rear tooth ≈ **2 mm**

## Care

Clean at the start of every race day and after off-track. Degrease, dry, lube when the chain is warm. Inspect aluminium rear sprockets — hooked/sharp teeth go in the bin with the chain. Align pinion and sprocket (straight-edge or laser). Side load wastes power and can throw the chain.

The stopwatch decides the ratio, not paddock talk.
