# US sprint trackside (era-1996)

## Session method

Sort handling on a **practice day**. Race day is for small steps only — changing three things at once teaches nothing.

Practice loop: heat-in laps, then a timed run as the baseline, **7–8 laps**, 15–20 minutes to change **one** thing, repeat. His fastest laps were usually **4th or 5th**; find your own rhythm.

On asphalt (except overpowered dirt), do not pitch the kart sideways to steer. A sliding kart scrubs speed; a planted kart gains lengths through the same corner.

His **cold starting pressures for that book**: 20 psi rear / 15 psi front. Treat as historical. Modern starting points live in `tyre-setup`.

Work **entry and exit** separately. Kart corners happen too fast for mid-corner car language.

## Push / understeer

Fronts not biting. Check tyre age and compound **before** the chassis: old/hard fronts push until they heat; setup on old rubber then race on new = too much bite; setup on new then race on old = loose.

**Pushes both ways**

1. Fronts not over-inflated (era: ~15 psi, then −2 psi both fronts).
2. Widen rear track (~1" practice / ½" race-day steps).
3. Move both fronts in one spacer.
4. More forward weight or seat forward (notch the seat if needed).
5. Lower the front (raise spindles in the carriers). Reset toe after spindle height.

**Pushes one way only:** check pressure, stagger, spindle height. Move the **front wheel opposite the push** in on the spindle (pushes left → move RF in). Or add a little weight to the **outside-front** of that turn (pushes left → RF ballast). Dirt often used corner ballast this way.

**Entry push:** more front weight; widen rear; if it pushes into a left, the RR is too tight — space RR out or take RR weight forward. Also: more stagger, newer tyres, more toe-out, less front stagger, loosen front bumper.

**Exit push:** front weight still low; fronts hard/old (improves as they heat); fronts over-inflated (round patch); front track too wide — move fronts in; more RR pressure; add toe-in; add rear stagger; move LF out. Then same as entry; last, raise spindles and reset toe. Persistent one-side bite → tweaked frame.

## Oversteer vs loose rear

**Oversteer** here = fronts bite so hard the kart darts in and the rear comes around. Often shows as **front tyre wear** and a second of opposite-lock. Causes: low front pressure, soft front compound, front track too narrow, too much front weight. Fix the **rear first** until it is settled, then take front bite out, then put a little rear slip back if you want it.

**Loose rear** = backs out while the front still sticks. First move: **bring rear wheels in** (1" steps in practice). Narrower rear = more rear stick; wider rear = looser. Then drop rear pressure (era: do not chase below ~10 psi — look elsewhere). Add rear flex: loosen rear bumper or rotate torsion bar **flat**. Slightly loose on cold tyres off the corner can be OK — it often comes in as they heat. Tightening a cold kart can bicycle at the end.

- Loose **entry:** toe-in, narrow rear, more RF pressure, less RR pressure, less rear stagger, loosen rear bumper, lower rear axle.
- Loose **exit:** less rear stagger, RR in, less rear pressure, lower rear axle, newer/softer rears. Dry-slick: more even rear weight; raise the nose.

Low-power classes often want to be a bit looser off the corner than high-power classes.

## Hop, bicycle, veer, dart

**Hop / skitter** in a hard left: move RR out ~1". Hop in a right: move LR out. Or +2 psi on the **outside-rear** of that turn. Excess stagger can hop. One-corner hop may be a bump — walk the track; do not “setup” a ripple.

**Bicycle** (inside wheels up, too much bite): more track both ends; more pressure on the biting side; move RR out; harder compound; less frame flex.

**Veer** (hands-off, pulls left or right): stagger (asphalt wants ~¼" or less; ovals/dirt might run inches); dragging wheel bearing; sprung frame.

**Dart** (twitchy to small steer): check toe; move tie-rods **out** on the spindle arms to slow the steering.

## Dirt chapter (short)

Dirt success is tyre choice and stagger first. Low pressure can add grip until the sidewall rolls and the kart tracks off-line. Dry-slick + loose → more rear weight, not just more stagger. See `martin-tyres-era`.

## Adjustment effects (era numbers — tools)

| Change | Typical result |
|---|---|
| Wider front track | More push, slower/heavier steer, more rear transfer |
| Narrower front | More front bite, quicker steer, looser rear |
| Narrower rear | More rear side bite, more front push |
| Wider rear | Looser rear, more front bite |
| More stagger | Less bite, looser; measure hot and cold (growth) |
| Asphalt stagger start | 0, then +1/8–1/4" until times drop |
| Oval/dirt stagger start | ~1" and up |
| More rear % | More HP can carry more rear %; too much → push. Era asphalt hint 60/40 — **conflicts with modern 43/57** |
| Banked oval | More left %, less rear % |
| Stiffer frame / bar vertical | Less transfer, less rear bite |
| More RR offset (wheel out) | Looser on entry |
| Less RR offset (tucked) | Tighter entry, more drive off |
| Lower pressure | Bigger patch until sidewall collapses (fish-tail) |

Race day: one-variable only — pressure, stagger/location, a few pounds. Cold start **20/15 psi** is era, not modern.
