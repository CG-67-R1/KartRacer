# US sprint tyres (era-1996)

## What to read on the tyre

**Across-tread heat** (practice, as soon as you stop — cool-down + scales ruin race-day pyrometer work):

- Inside always hottest → too much **negative camber**.
- Session-to-session absolute temp is less useful than **tyre-to-tyre and across each tyre**.

**No pyrometer:**

- Smooth / shiny → running cool.
- Rubber piled on the surface → running hot.
- Target look: dull, fine, like fine emery.

Scraping heat-gun build-up is preferred if the layer is uneven (mix of your rubber and track rubber). It will often wear off, but the first laps — the important ones — will be inconsistent. Fix the **cause** (unload that corner) rather than only scraping.

## Age and softeners

Hard/old tyres slip when cold: loose rear + big front push, then come in. When it takes **4–5 laps** instead of 1–2 to come in, they are done. Commercial softeners were already **illegal at some tracks**; effect often gone after a couple of laps. Avoid shop solvents (toluene / xylene) — health risk. Prefer new rubber.

## Rain vs dirt tread

- **True rain:** full-depth circumferential channels plus swept laterals to the shoulders; soft compound (water cools the rubber). Mount so the **centre of the pattern hits first**. On a drying track, drive the wet patches or they overheat and die.
- **Dirt:** square/rectangular blocks (brick-wall). Rain can work on dirt; dirt blocks usually fail in standing water.
- Combo “rain/dirt” tyres: compound matters more than the marketing name.

## Stagger and balance

Asphalt road course: start near **neutral**, ideally **≤ ¼" stagger**. Oval / dirt: era typical **2–3"** — confirm class and tyre rules.

Balance matters more as speed rises (enduro more than a tight dust-bowl). Valve stems and mixed wheel-half hardware are the usual imbalance. Fronts matter more than rears on a live axle, but a bent axle will fake a tyre problem. Dirt: wash mud off the wheels every race.
